**2.0.21, the unified calendar (the founder's 14 Aug correction, from his phone with screenshots).** He eyeballed 2.0.20 live and reset the shape: the advanced mode's full-bleed white panel is DEAD. The calendar is ALWAYS the simple view's inset card, the title always on the page background, and the two modes collapse into one surface: title and graph icon on the page (the prev/next chevrons are gone from Plus, months move by swiping; free keeps its chevrons untouched), the calendar card wearing the grab line, the legend folding with the month, the graph in its OWN card below, never inside the calendar area, and the record streaming under everything from today. The grammar, in his words: the grab line expands or compresses the calendar and ONLY the calendar; swiping the graph up begins to tuck it behind the calendar while the calendar compresses, both tracking the finger, bouncing on release to whichever state is closer; the icon brings the graph out for the month being read (the calendar stays as it is) and pressing it while out is the same outcome as the tuck, hidden and compressed; swiping months carries the title and the graph with it and moves the record to the first of that month. One structural fix fell out of browsing it: the stream is now its own offsetParent (`position: relative`), because day offsets were silently measured from the screen and included the pinned block's current height, so date taps overshot by that height and the strip's highlight drifted days off the true top whenever the calendar folded. The suite's calendar block was rewritten to the new grammar (negative-controlled: the 2.0.20 build fails the unified asserts), and free's lock gained a no-grab-line check beside its kept chevrons. Then the founder caught the strip's day rings shaved at the top on his phone: every height in the fold chain was ROUNDED, and a real cell is (width - gaps)/7, e.g. 40.578px, so the rounded translate overshot by up to ~0.84px at today's week. The chain is now fractional end to end (a 0.25px epsilon stops measurement jitter re-rendering), asserted as geometry against the fold's own clip line and negative-controlled by putting the rounding back (clips 0.42 to 0.84px, caught; fractional, 0.000px both edges). 318/318.

**2.0.20, the strip shows the month being read (the 13 Aug /arena run on the advanced calendar).** The founder's ask was to browse the real app and make the advanced calendar exactly right, and the arena's probes found four defects the 314-check suite could not see, because its calendar asserts measured heights and titles and never WHICH DATES the strip holds. (1) Entering advanced while the pager was parked on an older month pinned that month's week under a today title, and (2) scrolling the record across a month boundary moved the title but never the strip: both fixed by one rule, the pager follows the anchor's month whenever the strip is compressed (the full calendar still swipes freely, and folding it re-syncs). (3) With the calendar open over the stream, swiping months left the title behind: open, the title now follows the grid; folded, it names the month being read. (4) The read day now wears the accent on the strip and travels with the scroll, the prototype's `.cell.on` that the 2.0.16 build had dropped (spec 4.5). Underneath them sat two latent faults: `adopt()` called `nav.remember` inside its setState updater, which is impure and spiralled to React's nested-update cap the moment an effect called it (updaters re-run on every render pass); and the pull-to-exit path left `draggingRef` latched true, so after one pull-exit the next advanced session had a dead scroll-spy and a record that never paged past day 21. Two device guards joined them: a `touchmove` claim so the browser cannot `pointercancel` the pull mid-gesture, and `overscroll-behavior-y: contain` on the scroller so pull-to-refresh never steals a top-of-record pull. Five new asserts, negative-controlled as a set (the pre-fix build fails exactly those five, 314/319), and one older assert tightened after it passed for the wrong reason while both title and strip sat wrongly on the current month. 319/319.

**2.0.18, the calendar tracks the finger (founder, 11 Aug, after feeling the prototype).** The advanced calendar stops being two states that swap and becomes one surface with two numbers, both able to sit anywhere between 0 and 1 because they follow a thumb: **t** is the calendar, from the anchor's week alone to the whole month, and **g** is the graph, from tucked under the calendar to fully out. Nothing animates on a class any more; heights are inline pixels driven by the gesture, and the only animation is the short settle after release, tweened in JS from wherever the finger left it.

The week strip component is gone. It could not stretch into the month grid, so the compressed calendar IS the month grid, folded to one row, with the grid sliding so the week left standing is the one being read. Row height is MEASURED off a real cell rather than assumed, because cells are aspect-ratio squares whose size follows the screen and the parent's text-size setting.

The calendar is now a **panel**: full width, rounded where it meets the screen edge, a hairline under it, and **a grab line about a third of the Android home indicator that is the control, not decoration**. Drag it and the month stretches one to one; let go past halfway and it settles open. At the top of the record, pulling down brings the graph out with the finger, and pulling clean past it puts the month back, which is the way out of advanced mode: one continuous gesture, so a parent never learns two. The graph is the real one, four bars and the summary sentence, on the founder's instruction ("make sure you keep the current graph"). A day that holds something says so in the accent.

Two bugs the build found, both worth keeping in mind: **stretching the calendar moves the record, and the browser reports that as a scroll**, which made the "a scroll folds the month away" rule slam it shut the instant it opened (only a real change of scrollTop counts now, and never during a gesture); and **a drag that ends on a button still fires a click**, so releasing the grab line was calling the tap handler and folding it back. Suite 313/313, negative-controlled by making the handle ignore the finger: exactly the three tracking asserts failed.

**2.0.16, the advanced calendar: the Month tab learns a second mode (Bupe, 11 Aug).** Plus only, and free is untouched by design, not by omission ("in the free version how it is right now is unchanged"), which is its own assert. The founder's model, and it is better than the one Vision proposed: **the calendar's state is driven by deliberate gestures and discrete modes, never by scroll offset**. Swipe the graph UP and it tucks under the calendar, the month compresses to a pinned week strip, and the record streams in from the bottom; pull it DOWN and the month comes back. The graph icon does the same by tap and unfolds the graph from under the strip. Tapping the month and year opens the full calendar OVER the stream, with an arrow that turns; tapping a date there scrolls the record to it (paging in however many days it takes), and the next real scroll compresses the calendar again. As the parent scrolls, the highlighted day and the month title travel with them.

**Today is at the top and scrolling down goes back in time** (Bupe's call, asserted). That direction is not taste: Jotla refuses future-dated entries, so the empty days a parent can tap and fill can only lie in the past. Each bare day offers the note preset to ITS date. Paging is 21 days at a time, floored at the data epoch, with an honest end line rather than an infinite spinner.

Architecture: ONE vertical scroller owns the screen (the stream); the chrome is pinned outside it, never sticky inside it. The month grid is never re-laid-out, only re-slotted, so the pager's pixel-stable six-row panels survive. The scroll-spy is suppressed for 400-600ms after a scroll WE caused, or a tap-driven jump feeds back into the strip and fights itself. **Mode, graph and place ride the view via `nav.remember`**, found by the suite: opening a note from the record and coming back was dropping the parent out of advanced mode and losing their place, exactly the defect `remember` exists to prevent. Gestures use real non-passive listeners reading REFS, not state closures.

## The one-line direction

The shell goes neutral and the data gets the colour back. One accent (Jotla blue) does all the shell work; the mood colours are the only other colour on screen, so meaning is the brightest thing.

## Rules (all decided 2026-08-06, Bupe)

- **Rows, not cards.** Flat full-width rows on a slightly lighter surface, 14px radius, no card-in-card, no shadows doing border work. Light theme uses a hairline border instead of elevation.
- **One big left-aligned title** per screen ("Good afternoon.", "August 2026"). No app header bar; the wordmark's homes are the splash and About (per the 4 Aug declutter).
- **Section headers** are small, semibold, accent-coloured ("Check in", "Sam's day so far").
